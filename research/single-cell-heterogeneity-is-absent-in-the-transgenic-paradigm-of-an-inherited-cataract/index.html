Single Cell Heterogeneity is Absent in the Transgenic Paradigm of an Inherited Cataract

Suraj P. Bhat, Rajendra K. Gangalum, Dongjae Kim, Serghei Mangul, Raj K. Kashyap, Zhou Xinxai, David Elashoff

Abstract

Abstract: Cataract, an opacity of the eye lens, is a major cause of blindness. Inherited cataracts are often associated with mutations in genes encoding lens crystallins. The lens consists of highly specialized fiber cells that are derived from lens epithelial cells. In the mammalian lens, the differentiation of epithelial cells into fiber cells is a complex process. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying the fiber cell differentiation and the basis of the lens transparency remain poorly understood. Here, we used single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) to profile the transcriptome of single fiber cells from a transgenic mouse model of inherited lamellar cataract (LC). This mouse model expresses a mutant form of human γD-crystallin (CRYGD). We analyzed the transcriptomes of 468 single fiber cells from the lenses of wild-type and LC mice. Surprisingly, we found no evidence of transcriptional heterogeneity among the single fiber cells in either the wild-type or the LC mice. The expression levels of crystallin genes and other fiber cell-specific genes were highly consistent across all analyzed cells. Our results suggest that the molecular programs underlying fiber cell differentiation and the maintenance of lens transparency are highly uniform, at least in the context of the transgenic model studied. These findings provide new insights into the molecular basis of lens development and disease and suggest that single-cell heterogeneity may not be a universal feature of all cell types.
